# GENCO as a verifier of the corrective plans

Question: the corrective plan (redispatch + minimum load shedding) is computed by the backend and checked by the AC power flow. Can a model trained **only on topologies**
(no redispatch and no load shedding in the training data) tell, in milliseconds, whether a plan solves the overloads?

Result in `interface/src/data/verificacao_planos_case118_ieee_n10.json` (our N-10; the one for the authors' checkpoint, which the interface shows, is in `..._autores.json`); generated by `backend/tools/genco_verifica_planos.py` with **GENCO Tiny N-10**
(`models/case118_genco_tiny_n10`). The numbers below are those of that file.

## How the test is done
For each outage of **one line** that requires action (89 contingencies; the 9 that island buses are left out, GENCO did not see islands) we build states with the line out:

| State | What it is |
|---|---|
| no plan | only the automatic generation response (proportional redistribution) |
| partial plan | halfway between "no plan" and the LP plan |
| plan with margin *f* | LP plan computed to stay *f* points below the limit (*f* = 0, 3, 5, 8, 10; 0 is the exact plan of the panel) |

That makes 623 states. The **AC is the ground truth**. GENCO and the DC receive exactly the same injections (dispatch, loads with shedding, line out) and estimate the loading
of each line. "Approving" a plan = estimated maximum loading ≤ 100% of the limit.

## Results
- **Error per line** on states GENCO never saw: **1.03 % points** (DC 2.92). It is the same level as the validation without redispatch (0.93).
- **Insufficient plan approved by mistake** (173 states in which the AC flags an overload): **GENCO 0**, DC 121 (with a 100% threshold).
  With zero errors in 173, the upper bound of the error rate, at 95% confidence, is about 1.7% (rule of three).
- **Approval of safe plans** (100% threshold):

| Plan margin | Plans approved by GENCO | Total shedding | Mean redispatch | AC, mean maximum loading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (exact) | 3 of 89 | 227 MW | 56 MW | 99.9% |
| 3 points | 45 of 89 | 263 MW | 80 MW | 97.1% |
| **5 points** | **75 of 89** | 289 MW | 97 MW | 95.1% |
| 8 points | 82 of 89 | 327 MW | 133 MW | 92.3% |
| 10 points | 82 of 89 | 367 MW | 157 MW | 90.5% |

- **Comparison with the DC at the same safety level** (no insufficient plan approved): GENCO approves with a 100% threshold and a 5-point margin (75 of 89).
  The DC is only free of errors with a 95% threshold and then needs the 10-point margin (approves 78 of 89), which costs more (shedding 367 vs 289 MW; mean redispatch 157 vs 97 MW).

## What this shows and what it does not
- **It shows:** GENCO is a more reliable safety filter than the DC for plans with redispatch and shedding, even without having seen those states in training.
  With a moderate margin it clears most plans without going through the AC, and no insufficient plan got through.
- **It does not show** that GENCO replaces the AC. The exact plan sits at 100% of the limit and GENCO's typical error on the maximum loading is 2 to 4 points (median;
  p90 4 to 8; maximum 43 points in one contingency), with an upward bias: it overestimates, which makes it conservative. That is why the final plan is still confirmed by the AC.
- Limits: one network, one model, one training seed; only line outages that do not island buses (89 contingencies); the DC's 95% threshold was chosen looking at
  the same data (it favors the DC); on CPU GENCO (19 ms per state) is not faster than the AC (about 18 ms) on this small network.

## Where it appears in the interface
- **Corrective response** panel: when the fault is exactly one line, the "GENCO verifies the plan" block shows AC, GENCO and DC for three states
  (no plan, exact plan, plan with a 5-point margin) and the GENCO verdict.
- **N-1 screening** panel: block with the overall summary of this page.

## With the authors' checkpoint
The same test with the authors' GENCO Tiny gives an error per line of 0.21 % points (ours: 1.03) and approves 83 of 89 plans with a 3-point margin, but at 100% it approves 17 insufficient plans (ours, 0) because it has almost no bias; with a 99% threshold it approved none: see `docs/GENCO_CHECKPOINT_AUTORES.md`.

## Reproduce
In the GENCO environment (`gridfm-graphkit` + `torch`), from the repository root (about 2 minutes on CPU):
```bash
python backend/tools/genco_verifica_planos.py --modelo models/case118_genco_tiny_n10 [--device cpu] [--dst <file.json>]
```
The tool uses the same scenario builder as the screening (`scenario_rows` in `backend/tools/genco_screening.py`) and the in-memory inference of `tools/genco_inferencia_exemplo.py`.
